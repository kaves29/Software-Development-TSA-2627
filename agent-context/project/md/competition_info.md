## Event and eligibility

Design, implement, test, and document software with educational or social value that addresses a societal need connected to the annual theme. Demonstrate functionality and explain the design and code.

- One team per chapter may participate.
- Presentation/interview participation is limited to **three members**, even though this development team has four.
- General rules specify two to six members unless an event says otherwise; members must be from the same chapter and affiliated with TSA.
- Register all members and designate a submission captain through the advisor for online submissions.
- Follow conference registration, attendance, identification-badge, and TSA competition-attire requirements. The supplied general rules limit each participant to six combined individual/team events.

## Deliverables and timing

- Submit accessible executable raw code through an online repository URL or a linked PDF of all code, as described in the supplied event excerpt.
- Submit the **Resources and AI Reflection PDF**, maximum **four pages**, using the applicable official form.
- The excerpt specifies **11:59 p.m. Eastern on a designated date in mid-May**. It does not supply an exact date; do not invent one.

## Originality, sources, and AI use

- Students must create and complete the work, do their own analysis, and make independent decisions. AI support must help learning rather than replace it.
- The supplied event rules permit GenAI subject to the general rules. Record actual use and resources for the required reflection, with clear attribution and discussion of effectiveness and ethics.
- Cite external ideas, text, images, and sound using a professional citation style. The supplied rules require written permission for copyrighted material used in an entry.
- Entries must start and finish during the current school year, be submitted for only one year and one competition, and be in English. Each participant/team submits one entry per event.
- The supplied rules prohibit plagiarism, falsification, and unfair advantage. They specify a 20% penalty for honor-statement violations and for failure to use a professional citation style; other rule breaches can have different consequences.
